**Introduction**

Finding a home shouldn’t be too complicated. You just need to find the right property, gauge the cost, verify its legitimacy, and move in, but for many Nigerians, it is a hellish process.

The search is defined by expensive houses, limited availability, unreliable listings, lying agents, potential roommate clashes, and a systemic lack of trust.

As part of the Vephla University Simulations Capstone Programme, our team set out to build a web-based platform to help users easily find apartments, empty rooms, and compatible roommates securely and efficiently. The platform was designed to address common housing challenges such as fraud, poor communication, lack of trust, and inefficient search processes.

This analysis draws on the Apartment, House-Sharing & Roommate Experience Survey, a primary survey designed to understand how Nigerians experience house hunting, shared apartments and digital housing platforms.


**Story of Data**

The dataset used in this project captures first-hand responses from individuals regarding their demographic characteristics, current living situations, housing experiences, and interactions with shared housing and digital housing platforms in Nigeria. The data serves as the foundation for understanding housing behaviours, challenges, and preferences, and it informs all analytical outputs generated in this project.

The data originates from a primary survey titled "Apartment, House-Sharing & Roommate Experience Survey”. This is primary data, not extracted from an external public repository or third-party database. It was collected directly from respondents and compiled internally into an Excel workbook. As such, the dataset reflects real user-reported experiences rather than inferred or system-generated data.

The survey covered several dimensions of the housing journey, including demographics, current living arrangements, rental and shared housing experiences, housing search challenges, stress, fraud, and perceptions of digital housing platforms.

Each respondent represents one row in the dataset, while each column represents a survey variable. Key variables include age group, gender, location, employment status, current living arrangement, length of stay in a city, previous rental or shared housing experience, housing search stress, and trust-related factors.

These features enable a comprehensive analysis of who the respondents are, how they live, the challenges they face, and how they interact with modern housing solutions.

There are several limitations; respondents reported their own experiences, and the data may contain recall or perception bias. The sample consists only of people who participated in the survey, so the findings cannot automatically be generalised to the entire Nigerian population.

**Recommendations**

Prioritise Trust and Verification Infrastructure: Digital housing platforms should implement and prominently display verified listings and user profiles, clear landlord and roommate authentication processes and transparent reporting and dispute resolution mechanisms

Segment Housing Solutions by Demographic Profiles: Platform features and housing solutions should be tailored based on age group, employment or student status.

Improve Housing Search Transparency and Information Quality: Standardise listing information (pricing, location, amenities, rules), reduce information asymmetry between housing seekers and providers.

Strengthen Shared Housing Compatibility Mechanisms: introduce structured roommate matching criteria, set clear expectations around shared responsibilities

Integrate Qualitative Feedback into Decision-Making: Use it to refine platform features, incorporate user language into communication strategies, and identify emerging concerns not captured by structured questions.

**Conclusion**

This project provides a data-driven examination of modern housing challenges, emphasising that affordability, trust, and transparency are inseparable from housing accessibility. By addressing these dimensions holistically, stakeholders can design more resilient, user-centric housing platforms and policies that better serve diverse populations.
